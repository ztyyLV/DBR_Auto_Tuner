dbr-autotune

Give it a folder of photos containing barcodes. It tries hundreds of reader settings and writes out the one that reads the most barcodes fastest, as a file you can load into the Dynamsoft Barcode Reader SDK from C++, .NET, Java, Python or mobile.

New here? What this tool is for, in plain words

The barcode SDK has hundreds of settings. Its factory defaults handle easy barcodes — clean black-on-white labels. They often fail completely on hard ones: dot-peened marks on metal, laser etching on dark glass, low contrast, curved surfaces, bad lighting.

Finding the settings that do work is normally manual trial and error. This tool does that search for you: it decodes your images over and over with different settings, keeps whatever reads more barcodes, then strips out everything that was not pulling its weight so the result is also fast.

You need: a folder of representative photos — ideally a few dozen covering the real variation you see in production (different lighting, angles, batches, dirt). You get: a settings file, plus a report telling you honestly which images still could not be read.

Your images

A whole folder is usually what you want. Subfolders are included. JPEG, PNG, BMP, TIFF, GIF, WebP and PDF all work; multi-page PDFs and TIFFs are scored one page at a time.

Words this page uses

    

      Open the glossary
      

        
Barcode type

        
Which barcode “language” a mark is written in — QR Code, DataMatrix,
            Code 39, and so on. The SDK calls these BF_…
            names. Also called a symbology.


        
Template

        
The settings file this tool produces. You hand it to the SDK and it reads
            barcodes your way instead of the factory default way.


        
Read rate
(recall)

        
Of the barcodes we believe are in your images, the share that got read.
            Careful: when you have not supplied correct answers, “believe are
            there” means “something managed to read at some point”, so images nothing
            ever read drop out of this number and flatter it.


        
Page coverage

        
Of every image you supplied, the share that produced a read.
            Nothing drops out of this one, so when the two disagree, this is the
            honest number.


        
Undetermined

        
An image where nothing was ever read. It either has no barcode, or has one
            nobody could crack — the tool genuinely cannot tell which, so it says so
            instead of guessing. Look at these by eye.


        
Mean / p95

        
Average time per image, and the time that 95% of images come in under.
            p95 is what matters for a production line: it is roughly your worst
            realistic case, ignoring freak outliers.


        
DPM

        
Direct Part Marking — a code stamped, etched or dot-peened straight onto a
            product rather than printed on a label. Much harder to read, and it needs
            a specific mode switched on.


        
Correct answers
(ground truth)

        
A list you supply of what each image should decode to. With it,
            scores are real; without it, the tool has to infer them and cannot spot a
            misread.


        
Set aside
(holdout)

        
Images deliberately kept out of the search, used at the end as a fresh
            test. If the score collapses on them, the settings were memorising your
            sample rather than solving the problem.


        
Cross-validate

        
Repeat the whole process several times, each time setting aside a different
            group. You get an average and a spread. A wide spread means the
            answer depends on luck, and you need more images.


        
Timeout

        
A hard limit on how long the reader may spend on one image before giving
            up. The tool sets this automatically to twice the slowest image it saw.
